In development · Dice auto-battler

One game, four worlds

You roll, combinations form, your squad fights its way down a road of encounters — and five schools of dice (sword, greatsword, hammer, orb, poison) give you builds worth chasing across a run.

Forest Band

Badger, hedgehog, otter, owl and frog defending their wood. Reads anywhere, in any language.

Jurassic Squad

Five small dinosaurs pushing through a prehistoric valley, a volcano on the horizon.

Night Shift

A crew of animal specialists cracking safes in a neon city — every die is a member of the crew.

Real in-engine frame, current build

The world is a variable, not a decision

Mechanics, balance, interface and animation stay identical across settings. What changes is small and bounded — five squad characters, five weapon sets, the first chapter's enemies, one backdrop, the die itself and a ten-colour palette.

So instead of betting the project on one art direction, we build the same game in four worlds — the fourth is Cute Spooky, friendly little monsters from a pumpkin town — and let the audience tell us which one it wants to live in.

Interactive art

A wall of living paint

A camera watches the floor. Your silhouette glows from inside the fluid and your movement drives the colour the way wind drives smoke. No sensors, no suits, no controllers — nothing to learn.

✦ Try it in your browser

Interactive Effects

The world changes character every minute — water, smoke, fire, ink, glass, galaxy, mandala — and at the end of the cycle every drop of paint drains together and freezes into a logo, or into a QR code a phone can scan.

  • Cheap to install: a webcam, an ordinary computer and a projector or screen.
  • Venues author their own worlds in a built-in visual editor.
  • Scales to the wall — multi-monitor output driven at 15360×2160.
  • Run by non-technical crews: one-click installers, manuals, arrow-key calibration.
